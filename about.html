What it is

One Look at Who's Up.

upwind is a live map of Michigan's general aviation. It shows the planes in the air, the conditions at every airport that reports them, and the patterns that build up over days and weeks.

Why

General Aviation Matters.

Michigan's general aviation runs on small fields, flight schools, mechanics and people who simply love to fly. It moves medical flights, trains future airline pilots and connects towns that a highway never reaches. When nobody sees that activity, it's easy to overlook, and fields that get overlooked can close.

upwind exists to show how much flying really happens here. Every plane on the map makes the case for the airports that keep it going, and might be the thing that gets someone new to visit a field, take a discovery flight or start lessons.

Where the data comes from

Public, Live and Volunteer-Built.

  • Traffic from adsb.lol, with airplanes.live as a backup: networks of volunteer ADS-B receivers.
  • Weather reports and forecasts from the NOAA Aviation Weather Center.
  • Clouds and radar from NOAA satellite and NEXRAD imagery, served by the Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
  • Airports, airspace and the sectional chart from the FAA. Terrain from NOAA.
  • Airline routes from adsbdb, community route data that can be out of date.
How the numbers work

Flights and Landings.

upwind checks the receivers every 5 minutes. A flight is one plane's trip, from when it shows up in the air until it lands or goes quiet for 20 minutes. A landing is a flight that ended low over a Michigan airport.

Some flying is always missed: touch-and-goes inside one flight, short hops between checks, very low arrivals at small fields, and planes without ADS-B. So every count on upwind is a minimum.

Good to know

Privacy and Safety.

No accounts, no ads and no tracking. Your home airport and settings stay in your browser.

Aircraft whose owners have asked to keep their registration private are still shown and counted, but never named.

upwind is for fun and awareness only. It is not for navigation or flight planning. Use current charts, NOTAMs and an official briefing.
